BigCell: Generating Gigapixel Whole-Slide Images

NeurIPS 2026

Srikar Yellapragada1 Alexandros Graikos1 Zilinghan Li2 Kostas Triaridis1
Tarak Nath Nandi2,3 Karen Bai1 Beatrice Knudsen4 Tahsin Kurc1 Prateek Prasanna1
Rajarsi R. Gupta1 Ravi K. Madduri2,3 Joel Saltz1 Dimitris Samaras1

1Stony Brook University, 2Argonne National Laboratory, 3University of Chicago, 4University of Utah



We present BigCell, the first approach to generate coherent whole-slide histopathology images at gigapixel resolution. Existing histopathology generative models produce tiles of at most 1024x1024 pixels, a small fraction of a whole-slide image (WSI), while the supervision that exists at scale (pathology reports and slide-level labels) describes the entire slide. BigCell trains a flow-matching transformer at 2048x2048 pixels on over 120,000 publicly available WSIs, and generates coherent images of up to 32,768x32,768 pixels. We also propose the first text-to-image synthesis at 8k resolution, and show that synthetic data improves slide-level few-shot classification by up to 18%.

All images above are synthetic. Left: 32,768x32,768 images generated by BigCell, with 2048x2048 crops showing realism both at the macro-scale and at the cellular level. Right: two 8192x8192 images generated per pathology report.


Method​

(a) Conditioning. We control generation with a dense grid of UNI-2 embeddings, one per 256x256 patch. The grid is either extracted from a real WSI, or sampled from a pathology report by our conditioning generation model (CGM), a flow-matching transformer that operates directly in the UNI representation space.

(b) Tile generator. BigCelltile is a flow-matching transformer that generates 2048x2048 tiles in the latent space of the FLUX.2 VAE, conditioned on an 8x8 grid of UNI-2 embeddings. We train it progressively from 256x256 up to 2048x2048 resolution.

(c) Sliding-window generation. To go beyond the native resolution, we run the tile generator on overlapping windows of the global latent canvas and average their velocities into a global velocity field at every denoising step. The conditioning grid acts as a global blueprint, dictating what tissue morphology belongs where.


Comparison at 8k resolution​

We plug prior tile generators into the same sliding-window pipeline and compare against large-image methods at 8192x8192 resolution. BigCell achieves the highest slide-level similarity to the reference images under both TITAN and MOOZY, and leads on patch-level similarity, especially on the bottom-5th-percentile (tail) metrics, indicating far fewer regions of degraded local fidelity.

Efficient gigapixel generation​

Generating a 32k image requires denoising hundreds of overlapping windows at every step. Not all windows need the same effort: complex regions rely heavily on the conditioning, while background or homogeneous stroma are mostly shaped by the unconditional prior. We measure each window's importance as the difference between its conditional and unconditional velocities, which is available for free with classifier-free guidance:

Iij(t)=∥vθ(zij,t,yij)−vθ(zij,t,∅)∥1I_{ij}(t) = \| v_{\theta}(z_{ij}, t, y_{ij}) - v_{\theta}(z_{ij}, t, \emptyset) \|_{1}Iij​(t)=∥vθ​(zij​,t,yij​)−vθ​(zij​,t,∅)∥1​

At each step we only denoise the top-rrr most important windows, and reuse cached velocities for the rest. This is training-free, controlled by a single hyperparameter rrr, and focuses compute on tissue rather than background.

Adaptive scheduling beats simply using fewer sampling steps at every compute budget. At r=0.25r=0.25r=0.25 it matches full-compute quality (TITAN similarity 0.951 vs 0.957) with a 3.1x speedup, reducing 32k generation from 15.6 hours to 4.97 hours on a single GPU.


Text-to-image generation at 8k​

Synthetic 8192x8192 image generated from a pathology report, showing extensive pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.

Combining the CGM with BigCell, we generate 8192x8192 images directly from free-form pathology reports. On 150 held-out reports, generated images approach the text-image alignment of real paired data under both TITAN and PRISM, and match real data on TITAN recall@5.

Pathologist evaluation. Board-certified pathologists match synthetic images to their source reports as accurately as real ones (0.57 for both). When asked to tell real from synthetic, they labeled synthetic images as real 55% of the time, and their inter-rater agreement was at chance (Cohen's κ = 0.020).


Synthetic data augmentation​

The CGM also enables class-consistent data augmentation: we noise the UNI grid of a real training image, denoise it under a class-specific synthetic report, and render it with BigCell. Augmenting the real training set improves few-shot classification on TCGA-UT-8K and PANDA across all shot counts, with relative gains of up to 18% on PANDA (which is held out from CGM training).
